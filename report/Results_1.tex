\section{Comparison of outputs}

The three comparison runs achieved outputs that are in general agreement with respect to CWP location and shape, but are not identical at the pixel level. The Jaccard similarities for the three runs are 0.936, 0.956, and 0.976, indicating generally good agreement between v1 and v2.

Figure~\ref{fig:param_combi_3_general_agreement} shows the CWPs generated during trial run 3, with polygons of the speed-optimized algorithm (v2) shown in red and polygons of the original algorithm (v1) shown in blue. The two algorithms are in general agreement, however do not fully agree at the pixel level. Figure~\ref{fig:trial_run_1_more_pronounced_dis} shows a more pronounced example of this pixel-level disagreement for trial run 1, while Figure~\ref{fig:trial_run_perfect_agreement} shows an example of perfect agreement between the two methods.

\section{Speed comparison in execution}

Figure~\ref{fig:speed_comparison} shows the execution time of v1 and v2 as a function of segment length. The execution time of v2 is approximately 0.5 hours across all three tested segment lengths, remaining largely constant. In contrast, v1 shows execution times ranging from approximately 2 hours at a segment length of 400~m to approximately 1.25 hours at 1200~m, with execution time increasing as segment length decreases (i.e., as the number of segments increases).

\begin{figure}[ht]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Bilder/speed_comparison.png}
	\caption{Execution time of the original (v1) and optimized (v2) implementations of \texttt{detect\_cwp\_single} as a function of segment length. The optimized implementation shows execution times that are both shorter and largely constant across segment lengths, whereas the original implementation scales disproportionately with decreasing segment length (i.e., increasing number of segments).}
	\label{fig:speed_comparison}
\end{figure}


\section{Sensitivity scores for step length parameter}

Figure~\ref{fig:step_length_sensitivity} shows the Morris sensitivity indices $\mu^*$ and $\sigma$ with respect to the step length parameter, stratified by CWP class ($n = 10$ tributary-caused, $n = 14$ non-tributary-caused). All non-tributary-caused CWP show $\mu^*$ values below 0.2, indicating low sensitivity to the step length parameter across this class. The tributary-caused CWP show a wider spread in $\mu^*$: some tributary-caused CWP have sensitivity values comparable to the non-tributary-caused group, while others show substantially higher values of up to 0.3--0.6. The median $\mu^*$ values of the two classes are similar, with the primary difference lying in the upper tail of the tributary-caused distribution. The $\sigma$ values show a comparable pattern, with greater spread in the tributary-caused group.

\begin{figure}[ht]

	\label{fig:step_length_sensitivity}
\end{figure}

\section{Overall $\mu^*$ across all parameters}

Figure~\ref{fig:mu_star_overall} displays the $\mu^*$ values for all 24 CWP locations with respect to the three parameters varied in the second Morris experiment. The buffer pixel count shows consistently low $\mu^*$ values across all locations, with the entire distribution concentrated near zero. In contrast, both step length and temperature delta show distributions that deviate clearly from zero. The temperature delta parameter shows the highest $\mu^*$ values overall, suggesting it has the strongest influence on the detected CWP area across the set of annotated locations.

\section{$\mu^*$ (step length) versus CWP area}

Figure~\ref{fig:mu_star_vs_area} shows the 24 selected CWP along three dimensions simultaneously: mean CWP area averaged across all Morris runs (x-axis), $\mu^*$ with respect to the step length parameter (y-axis), mean temperature delta to the surrounding environment averaged across all Morris runs (color), and CWP class (shape). The data suggest a positive relationship between mean CWP area and sensitivity to the step length parameter: locations with larger mean CWP area tend to show higher $\mu^*$ values. This pattern appears across both classes. No clear relationship between mean temperature delta and $\mu^*$ is visible from the coloring.

Table~\ref{tab:regression_mustar} shows the results of the linear model fitted to predict $\mu^*$ (step length) from mean CWP area and mean temperature delta. The mean area predictor is highly significant ($p < 0.001$) with a positive slope, consistent with the visual pattern in Figure~\ref{fig:mu_star_vs_area}. The mean temperature delta predictor is not significant ($p = 0.556$), indicating no detectable linear relationship between temperature delta and sensitivity to the step length parameter after accounting for area. The model explains a substantial proportion of variance ($R^2 = 0.611$, Adjusted $R^2 = 0.572$).

\end{figure}
