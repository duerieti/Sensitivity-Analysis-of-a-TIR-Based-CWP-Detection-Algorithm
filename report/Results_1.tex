\section{Algorithm Reimplementation}
\label{sec:rq1_results}

The following two subsections present the runtime benchmarking and output 
equivalence results for v1 and v2.

\subsection{Runtime Comparison}

Figure~\ref{fig:speed_comparison} shows the execution time of v1 and v2 as a 
function of step length. The execution time of v2 is approximately 0.5 hours 
across all three tested step lengths, remaining largely constant. In contrast, 
v1 shows execution times ranging from approximately 2 hours at a step length 
of 400~m to approximately 1.25 hours at 1200~m, with execution time increasing 
as step length decreases (i.e., as the number of segments increases).

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Bilder/speed_comparison.png}
	\caption{Execution time of v1 and v2 as a function of step length. In red the original CWP detection algorithm (v1) in blue the speed optimized algorithm (v2).}
	\label{fig:speed_comparison}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Output Equivalence}

The three comparison runs achieved Jaccard similarities of 0.936, 0.956, 
and 0.976, indicating generally good spatial agreement between v1 and v2. 
Figure~\ref{fig:v1v2_comparison} illustrates the range of agreement observed. 
Panel (a) shows a representative example of general agreement, with 
discrepancies confined to a small number of boundary pixels. Panel (b) shows 
a more pronounced boundary mismatch. Panel (c) shows an example of complete 
spatial agreement, where the two polygon sets are visually indistinguishable.

\section{Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:rq2_results}


The Morris sensitivity analysis results are presented below across three 
complementary views, progressing from the specific hypothesis regarding 
step-length sensitivity to the broader parameter ranking and the 
relationship between CWP area and sensitivity.

\subsection{Sensitivity of CWP Area to Step Length}

Figure~\ref{fig:step_length_sensitivity} shows the Morris sensitivity indices 
$\mu^*$ and $\sigma$ with respect to the step length parameter, stratified by 
CWP class. All 
non-tributary-caused CWP show $\mu^*$ values below 0.2, indicating low 
sensitivity to the step length parameter across this class. The tributary-caused 
CWP show a wider spread in $\mu^*$: some tributary-caused CWP have sensitivity 
values comparable to the non-tributary-caused group, while others show 
substantially higher values of up to 0.3--0.6. The median $\mu^*$ values of the 
two classes are similar, with the primary difference lying in the upper tail of 
the tributary-caused distribution. The $\sigma$ values show a comparable pattern, 
with greater spread in the tributary-caused group.

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Bilder/step_length_results.png}
	\caption{Morris sensitivity indices ($\mu^*$ and $\sigma$) for step length 
		with respect to cold water patch area, stratified by patch class.}
	\label{fig:step_length_sensitivity}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Overall Sensitivity across all Parameters}

Figure~\ref{fig:mu_star_overall} displays the $\mu^*$ and $\sigma$ values for 
all 24 CWP locations with respect to the three parameters varied in the second 
Morris experiment. The buffer pixel count shows consistently low $\mu^*$ values 
across all locations, with the entire distribution concentrated near zero, and 
a similarly negligible $\sigma$, indicating neither a meaningful main effect nor 
relevant interactions with the other parameters. In contrast, both step length 
and temperature delta show distributions that deviate clearly from zero for both 
$\mu^*$ and $\sigma$. The temperature delta parameter shows the highest $\mu^*$ 
values overall, suggesting it has the strongest influence on the detected CWP 
area. For both step length and temperature delta, $\sigma$ is of comparable 
magnitude to $\mu^*$, pointing to the presence of nonlinearity or interactions 
with the other parameters for both.

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Bilder/mustar_sig_over_all.png}
	\caption{$\mu^*$ and $\sigma$ sensitivity indices for buffer pixel count, 
		step length, and temperature delta across all classified cold water 
		patches ($N = 24$ locations, $r = 64$ trajectories).}
	\label{fig:mu_star_overall}
\end{figure}


\subsection{Relationship between CWP Area and Step Length Sensitivity}

Figure~\ref{fig:mu_star_vs_area} shows the 24 CWP locations along three 
dimensions simultaneously: mean CWP area averaged across all Morris runs 
(x-axis), $\mu^*$ with respect to the step length parameter (y-axis), mean 
temperature delta averaged across all Morris runs (color), and CWP class 
(shape). Locations with larger mean CWP area tend to show higher $\mu^*$ 
values. No clear pattern between mean temperature delta and $\mu^*$ is 
visible from the colouring.

Table~\ref{tab:regression_mustar} shows the results of the linear model. 
The mean area predictor is highly significant ($p < 0.001$) with a positive 
slope, consistent with the visual pattern in Figure~\ref{fig:mu_star_vs_area}. 
The mean temperature delta predictor is not significant ($p = 0.556$). 
The model explains a substantial proportion of variance ($R^2 = 0.611$, 
Adjusted $R^2 = 0.572$).

\begin{figure}[ht]

		shape indicates CWP class (circle: non-tributary-caused; triangle:

	\label{fig:mu_star_vs_area}
\end{figure}

\section{Algorithm Modification}
\label{sec:rq3_results}

same Jaccard similarity metric applied in Section~\ref{sec:output_equivalence}. 
The Jaccard similarities obtained were 0.325, 0.334, and 0.287 -- substantially 
lower than the values of 0.936, 0.956, and 0.976 obtained for v2 versus v1.
