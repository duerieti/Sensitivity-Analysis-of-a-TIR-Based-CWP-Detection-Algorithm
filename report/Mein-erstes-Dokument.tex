	\section*{Abstract}
	
Cold water patches (CWPs) provide thermal refugia for cold-water organisms 
and are expected to gain ecological importance as river temperatures rise due to 
climate change. In a survey conducted in collaboration with the Canton of Bern, 
high-resolution TIR orthophotos were acquired along a 69~km transect of the 
Emme river, and a newly developed algorithm was deployed to detect CWPs 
programmatically. While the algorithm produced plausible results, output was 
found to vary with the parameters supplied, and detected patches were 
occasionally truncated or severed in a manner attributable to the algorithm's 
internal segmentation of the river reach. The truncation mechanism suggested 
that tributary-caused CWPs, which tend to be larger and more elongated, might 
be disproportionately affected by the step length parameter relative to 
non-tributary-caused CWPs.

To investigate this hypothesis and to characterise the sensitivity of the 
algorithm more broadly, a Morris sensitivity analysis was conducted. Since this 
required hundreds of repeated algorithm executions, a speed-optimized 
GDAL-based reimplementation was first developed and validated against the 
original algorithm using the Jaccard similarity index, achieving similarities 
of 0.936--0.976 across three validation runs while reducing execution time by 
a factor of three to four. The Morris analysis was then conducted across 24 
manually selected and classified CWP locations, varying buffer pixel count, 
step length, and temperature delta. Temperature delta was identified as the 
most influential parameter, followed by step length, while buffer pixel count 
showed negligible influence. Step-length sensitivity was confirmed to be higher 
in tributary-caused CWPs, with CWP size identified as the proximate driver. A modified algorithm replacing the segment-based reference temperature 
computation with an inverse distance weighting scheme eliminates the step length 
parameter and reduces the truncation artefacts observed in the original 
implementation.
