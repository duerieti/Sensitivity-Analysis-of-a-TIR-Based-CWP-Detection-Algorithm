\section{Answering research question 1}

The boxplots show that there is a tenedeny that the tributary caused cwps are more sensitive to the step length parameter than the non tributary-caused. The mean of the boxplot is slightly higher and the third quantile aswel ass the maximal value is significicanyl higher. Upon closer expection however it is visible, that some tributary caused cwp behave very much alike the non tributary cwps in their sensitivity while a second group seems to be vastly more sensitive. It is therefore questionable, whether the pure identitiy of beeing caused by a tributary is telling the whole story when it comes to governing sensitivity to step length or whether there are further effects at play within the tributary-caused group that ultimately govern the sensitivity with respect to the buffer length. In other words, beeing caused by a tributary could be an important prerequisite of beeing senitive to the step length parameter, however it is not the complete and ultimate cause of the sensitivity itself.

And this interpretation can find support in the scatter plot. Given by the coloring reflecting the temperature difference of the cwp to the surrounding stream, it seems that both high deltaT cwp aswell as low deltat cwp seem to be insensitive to the step length parameter. Additionally there are also certain cwp with intermediate sensitivity to the step length parameter ( mu star of 0.3) which have both high aswell as smaller temperature deltas. So by the gnerall mixing of colors on the plot, it does not seem like the temperature delta has not much if an influence on whether a cwp is sensitive to the step lenght parameter. The data however suggests that higher cwp areas drive the sensitivity to the step length parameter. To be more specific the data seems to suggest a linear relationship between mean cwp area and the step lenght sensitivity. This visual interpreation is also supported by the linear model fitted to the data. The slope derived for the mean\_area is highly significant while the slpe derived for the mean temperature delta is not significant with a p value of 0.55 indicating that this value is likely to be observed when no correlation between the temperature delta and the sensitivity to step length is assumed. An important caviat to take into account is the small sample size of n=24. While the highgly significant parameter for the cwp area on the on hand supports, that the linear relation is strong and allready detectable with a small number of parameters, it is still also important to understand, that the chance of accidentaly sampling a linear relation when a small number of points are sampled is higher, then when a large number of smaples are drawn. 

But generally the data would support the following claim: CWP caused by tributaries are not automatically highly sensitive to to the step length parameter. What really counts is the size of the CWP. Beeing tributary caused seems merely to be a driver to beeing a large CWP, since a tributary might introduce large amounts of colder water into the river which in turn could be a prerequisite for large CWPs. So beeing tributary caused can be a good driver for a cwp to be large. And large cwps are mainly long, this because the emme is a river and rivers are usually much longer than they are broad so in order to have a large are, a patch generally needs to be long while gaining large are from becoming more broad is limited. So large cwps have a tendency to be long and if a cwp is long then the chance that the border between two slaps falls whithin the patch is larger. and this is then the drive in the suceptability to the step length parameter. falling into different slabs as this yields different reference temperatues which are used for comparing.

Form the data it can also not be said whether a large non tributary-caused cwp would be equaly succeptible to the step length as equaly large tributary-caused cwp. This because there is no non tributary caused cwp larger than 1000 m\^2 in the data present.

Interestingly the temperature difference and the area of the cwp seem not to be related. This is rather interesting since I authomatically assumed that a cwp can potentialy grow larger if a tributarry provides cold water.
